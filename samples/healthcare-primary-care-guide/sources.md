# Sources

The article links to the following references beside the statements they support. These sources were consulted while preparing the article on 3 November 2026; the National Institute on Aging pages were available through indexed excerpts when direct access was blocked.

1. [MedlinePlus: Choosing a primary care provider](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001939.htm). Supports the description of primary care and finding a provider suited to ongoing care.
2. [General Medical Council: Our registers](https://www.gmc-uk.org/registers). Supports the UK example of checking a doctor's professional registration.
3. [National Institute on Aging: 17 questions to ask when choosing a new doctor](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/17-questions-ask-when-choosing-new-doctor). Supports questions about access, scheduling, language, and practice arrangements.
4. [MyHealthfinder: Choosing a doctor](https://odphp.health.gov/myhealthfinder/doctor-visits/regular-checkups/choosing-doctor-quick-tips). Supports confirming insurance participation with the clinic and insurer.
5. [National Institute on Aging: How to choose a doctor you can talk to](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/how-choose-doctor-you-can-talk). Supports communication, contact methods, and telehealth questions.

The US sources and UK register illustrate general provider-selection considerations. Check local registration, payment, and access arrangements before adapting the article for a specific country.

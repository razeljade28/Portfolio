# How to choose a primary care doctor: A practical guide

> Practice project / Healthcare writing sample

To choose a primary care doctor, check their qualifications and whether they provide the care you need. Find out what appointments cost and whether you can attend them. When you meet, notice how comfortable you feel talking about your health.

## What care do you need?

A primary care provider helps with common health concerns, preventive care, and referrals to specialists. You may see them over many years, so think about the care you're looking for beyond your first appointment. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001939.htm)

Write down who needs care and what matters to you. You might be looking for a doctor for your child or someone experienced in caring for older adults. If you have an existing condition, ask whether the doctor regularly manages it and how they work with other healthcare professionals. Your current provider may be able to recommend someone.

## Check the doctor's registration

Find the doctor's full name and professional qualifications, then check their registration with the official medical regulator where they practise.

In the UK, the General Medical Council has a public register showing doctors' registration status and other professional information. For care elsewhere, use the regulator for that country or region. [General Medical Council](https://www.gmc-uk.org/registers)

Make sure the record belongs to the doctor you plan to see. If you can't find a match, ask the clinic for their registration number and contact the regulator for help.

## Check appointment times and access

Call the clinic to check whether the doctor accepts new patients and how far ahead you need to book. Ask about opening hours and who provides care when the doctor is away.

Work out how long the journey will take and whether transport or parking is available. Check that the building meets your accessibility needs, and ask about language support if you need it. These are among the questions the National Institute on Aging recommends asking when choosing a doctor. [Questions to ask a new doctor](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/17-questions-ask-when-choosing-new-doctor)

If you need to take time off work or arrange transport for each visit, include that in your comparison.

## Ask what you'll pay

Ask for the price of the first consultation and what's included. Check whether tests, procedures, or follow-up appointments cost extra.

If you have insurance, check your coverage with the insurer and confirm that the clinic accepts your specific plan. Do this even if the doctor appears in your insurer's directory. [MyHealthfinder](https://odphp.health.gov/myhealthfinder/doctor-visits/regular-checkups/choosing-doctor-quick-tips)

You could ask: "What will I pay for the first appointment? Is there anything else I might be charged for?"

Payment arrangements vary by location and healthcare system. Keep a note of the answer and when you confirmed it.

## Notice how the doctor communicates

During the first appointment, notice whether the doctor listens and explains unfamiliar terms. You should feel able to ask questions or say that you need something explained again. The National Institute on Aging recommends considering that comfort when choosing a doctor. [Choosing a doctor you can talk to](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/how-choose-doctor-you-can-talk)

Afterward, ask yourself whether your main concerns were addressed and whether you understand the next steps. Think about whether you'd feel comfortable discussing something sensitive with this doctor, too.

## Check what happens between visits

Ask how you'll receive test results and how to contact the clinic with questions. If you want telephone or online consultations, check whether they're available and what they cost. [National Institute on Aging](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/medical-care-and-appointments/how-choose-doctor-you-can-talk)

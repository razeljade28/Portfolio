# How to prepare for a doctor's appointment

Before your next doctor's appointment, write down what you want help with and gather the information the clinic needs. Keep your notes somewhere you can reach during the visit, whether that's a notebook or your phone. Start with the concern you most want to discuss.

## Put your main concern first

Make a short list of questions, then decide which one matters most today. It might be a new symptom or a problem with your current treatment. Bring it up at the start of the conversation so it doesn't get left until you're about to leave. [NIH appointment guide](https://health.nih.gov/health-topics-a-z/prepare-for-appointment)

You can use a simple opening: "The main thing I'd like help with today is ____." If you have other concerns, mention that you have a list and ask which ones you can cover during this visit.

## Describe what you've noticed

Use your own words to explain the problem. Note when it began and anything that seems to ease it or make it worse. You can write these details before the appointment and refer to them when the doctor asks about your symptoms. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/talkingwithyourdoctor.html)

Be honest about your habits and what has changed. If you're unsure of a date or detail, say so. A note such as "I first noticed this around the start of last month" is more useful than feeling you have to supply an exact date you don't remember.

## Gather medicines and relevant records

Prepare a list of the medicines you take and their doses, including products bought without a prescription, vitamins, and herbal supplements. Bringing the containers is another option. Take relevant medical records if the doctor doesn't already have them, along with the contact details of other doctors you see. Bring your insurance card if it applies to your visit. [NIH appointment guide](https://health.nih.gov/health-topics-a-z/prepare-for-appointment)

Include any known allergies in your notes. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/talkingwithyourdoctor.html)

For a first visit, ask the clinic whether you can complete its medical history form beforehand. You'll have time to look up information at home. [NIH appointment guide](https://health.nih.gov/health-topics-a-z/prepare-for-appointment)

## Arrange communication support

If you need an interpreter, contact the clinic before the appointment to request one. You can also ask someone you trust to come with you and take notes while you talk. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/talkingwithyourdoctor.html)

Decide what help you'd like from that person. You might want a reminder about a question you planned to ask, or help keeping track of the answers.

## Check the plan before you leave

When the doctor recommends a test, ask what it is intended to find out. Confirm when to expect the result and whom to contact if it doesn't arrive. For treatment, ask about the available options, possible unwanted effects, and how you'll know whether it is helping. [NHS appointment questions](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/what-to-ask-your-doctor/)

If an explanation is unclear, ask the doctor to go over it again. You can check your understanding by saying what you think the next step is and asking whether you've got it right. Write down any follow-up arrangements and the contact details for questions after the visit. [NHS appointment questions](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/gps/what-to-ask-your-doctor/)

Tell the doctor if you expect difficulty following the plan. Ask how to prepare for any test they recommend, and request written instructions you can refer to later. [AHRQ patient guidance](https://www.ahrq.gov/questions/be-engaged/index.html)

## Keep your notes after the visit

Arrange any tests or specialist appointments you've been asked to book. If the instructions are unclear once you're home, contact the clinic for an explanation. Speak with your doctor or pharmacist before stopping a prescribed medicine. [AHRQ patient guidance](https://www.ahrq.gov/questions/be-engaged/index.html)
